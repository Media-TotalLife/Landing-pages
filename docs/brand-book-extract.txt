A N O T E F R O M N E E L A M
Why this exists
at all.
I
built Total Life because I couldn't find what my own
family needed. A mental health practice that took
Medicare. Clinicians who actually understood older
adults. Someone who would just pick up the phone when a
daughter called.
That practice didn't exist. Or — it existed in fragments,
behind eight-week waitlists, in clinics that didn't bill the
right way, with intake forms designed for somebody half
her age. The system wasn't broken. It had simply never
been built for her.
So this is what we're building. A national behavioral health
practice for older adults on Medicare. Therapy that's
covered. Psychiatry that's covered. Care navigation that's
free. Real people, not bots. A daughter who calls us and
gets a daughter back.
This brand book is the operating manual. Every page in it
answers to the same standard: would this have helped my
mom? If yes, it ships. If it makes my mom's life harder —
confusing copy, hidden phone numbers, sales-y urgency
on a senior — it doesn't. That's the filter. That's the brand.
Neelam Brar
FOUNDER & CEO · TOTAL LIFE

Who we are.
Why we exist.
Total Life supports aging adults and caregivers as they navigate the emotional
challenges of aging. Through Total Life, members get matched with a compassionate
therapist and meet virtually — helping them find joy, purpose, and improvement in their
wellbeing.
O R I G I N
Built by a daughter, for every daughter.
Total Life started because the founder
couldn't find adequate behavioral health
support for a family member inside the
Medicare system. That's not a marketing
story. It's the operating philosophy.
M I S S I O N
Mental health care for every older adult.
Make real behavioral health care accessible
to every older adult in America — covered by
Medicare, delivered at home, in language
that respects them.

from you. That's not metaphor — it's the operating principle. Every layout, color choice, and word should
feel like that moment of being able to exhale. We're not a hospital. We're not a startup. We're the feeling
of finally being met where you are.

1 3 · D E S I G N I N G F O R O L D E R A D U LT S
D E S I G N I N G F O R 6 5 +
Built for the way older adults
actually use things.
Most digital products are designed for the people who built them — fast typers, sharp
eyes, fluent in apps. Our members are not those people. These are the patterns that
make Total Life feel obvious, calm, and easy at any age.
Core principles
P R I N C I P L E 0 1
One thing per screen.
One question. One CTA.
One decision. Never two
columns of choices. Never
"while you're here, also…"
Older users get lost when
a screen does more than
one job.
P R I N C I P L E 0 2
Show, don't hide.
No hamburger menus on
critical paths. No "tap here
to reveal." Always show:
where you are, how to go
back, how to reach a
human, the phone
number. Hidden
navigation is hostile.
P R I N C I P L E 0 3
Generous time, soft
errors.
30-minute session
timeouts (not 5). Forgiving
inputs. Errors in plain
English: "Let's try that
again" — never "Invalid
format." A user who can't
recover from one error is
lost forever.
Pattern minimums
PATTERN
STANDARD
Body text

What we do.
What we never do.
Marketing to Medicare beneficiaries is regulated under the Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS),
the Civil Monetary Penalties Law (CMP), CMS marketing guidelines, and TCPA. These
rules govern every consumer-facing surface — paid, organic, partner, mail. Cross-
reference before any campaign ships.
✓ Do
✗ Don't
Describe what Medicare actually covers.
"Behavioral health is covered by Medicare.
Most members are covered up to 100% with
supplemental insurance."
✓
Call out free services Total Life provides.
"Our coverage check is free." "Initial care
navigation is free."
✓
Qualify coverage language carefully.
"Most members are covered up to 100%
with Medicare + supplemental insurance" —
specific, accurate, source-able.
✓
Make truthful outcome claims with
sources. "Most members feel hope after
three sessions (internal data, Q3 2024,
N=2,847)."
✓
Disclose Medicare provider status. "Total
Life is an enrolled Medicare provider."
✓
Lead with the founder origin story in
caregiver contexts. Caregiver-to-caregiver
framing. First person.
✓
Get legal sign-off same-day before any
new claim ships. Don't bottleneck the
work, but never skip it.
✓
Don't say "free therapy" or "free
sessions." AKS / CMP risk — Medicare
beneficiaries can't be induced with free
covered services.
✗
Don't say "no cost" without a
supplemental qualifier. Most members pay
something with original Medicare alone.
✗
No gifts, gift cards, raffles, or contest
entries for Medicare beneficiaries. The OIG
hard limit is $15 single / $75 annual.
✗
No paid patient referrals. Affiliate / content
partner programs use flat-fee sponsored

✗
No absolute outcome claims. "Will cure

with source.
✗
No fear or false urgency framing on
seniors. "Limited spots" / "Act now or lose

✗

official endorsement. Never use CMS,
Medicare, or HHS logos. Never imply we
are Medicare.
✗
No cold-calling. TCPA + CMS marketing

Three prompts.
Every piece, every time.
Before any Medicare-facing piece of marketing leaves the building, run it through these
three prompts. If any of them comes back wrong — reframe or kill the asset. Don't ship it
and hope.
T E S T 0 1
Inducement
Test
The prompt: "Does this
offer something of value to
a Medicare beneficiary to
influence them to use a
Medicare-reimbursable
service?"
If no → proceed. Most
truthful, value-based
marketing passes this.
If yes → reframe. Remove
the inducement. Or kill the
asset.
T E S T 0 2
Free-What Test
The prompt: "If we say
'free' anywhere, is it
specifically a service
Medicare doesn't cover —
or that Total Life is
independently providing
— and is that clear?"
If yes → proceed. "Our free
coverage check" is fine —
coverage checks aren't a
Medicare service.
If no → reframe. "Free
therapy" / "free sessions"
violate AKS / CMP. Use
"covered by Medicare"
instead.
T E S T 0 3
Substantiation
Test
The prompt: "Every
numerical claim, outcome
statement, and superlative
— can we cite a source
today, internally or
externally?"
If yes → proceed. Include
the source in fine print or
methodology page.
If no → kill the claim.
Substitute a sourceable
alternative. No source = no
claim.
